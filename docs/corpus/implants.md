# Cyberware and implants

Cyberware is the umbrella term for implanted technology: optical chips,
neural links, reinforced skeletons and synthetic organs. Around 40% of
Night City residents carry at least one implant, and in the Foundry
district the figure rises above 80%.

## Legal limits

The Utilities Commission's public-health code caps civilian cyberware at
35% of total body function replacement without a special licence. The cap
is measured by a standard assay called the Brava index. Enforcement is
uneven: Spire clinics file the paperwork, while Market back-alley surgeons
measure the index with a coin toss. Exceeding the Brava index without a
licence voids most insurance policies, which in Night City is a more
serious consequence than the fine.

## Neural implants

Neural implants interface directly with the nervous system. Optic chips
overlay augmented reality onto natural vision and are the most common
implant in the city. Cochlear threads restore or enhance hearing. Deep
links connect the motor cortex to a cyberdeck and are required for
netrunning. All deep links end in a neural socket at the base of the
skull, standardised as the MX-9 connector so that any cyberdeck can plug
into any runner.

## Rejection and maintenance

The body's immune response to implants is managed with suppressant
therapy, a weekly injection sold at every clinic. Missing doses causes
cyber-psychosis episodes: dissociation, aggression, and in advanced cases
the sensation that the body's own limbs are foreign objects. Implants also
require firmware patches, distributed over the data grid and signed with
corporate keys — which is why unlicensed implants stop receiving security
updates and are increasingly vulnerable to remote exploits.

## The Mirror series

The best-known consumer implant line is the Mirror series from Meridian
Systems: the Mirror-eye optic chip, the Mirror-spine posture controller
and the Mirror-hand fine-motor replacement. The line's slogan, "See
yourself improved", is painted on towers across the Market district.
