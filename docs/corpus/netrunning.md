# Netrunning

Netrunning is the practice of connecting a human nervous system directly to
the data grid using a cyberdeck. A netrunner jacks in through a neural
socket at the base of the skull and projects their consciousness into
network space, where corporate systems appear as geometric structures and
defensive software takes physical form.

## ICE — Intrusion Countermeasures Electronics

ICE is defensive software that guards corporate servers. The name is a
deliberate joke among engineers: hostile intruders are said to be "on thin
ice". Basic ICE simply blocks access, but military-grade ICE — nicknamed
black ICE — traces intruders back through their connection and floods their
nervous system with feedback, causing anything from seizures to death.
Corporations layer ICE walls around their most valuable data vaults, and
the thickness of the wall is usually a good proxy for the value of what
lies behind it.

## Daemons and breach tooling

A daemon is a self-executing program a netrunner carries on their cyberdeck.
Common daemons include the ping daemon, which reveals the layout of nearby
subnets, the mask daemon, which spoofs a friendly credentials handshake, and
breach daemons, which carve an opening in ICE long enough to exfiltrate
data. Daemons are written in a language called KOBOL, a descendant of COBOL
that compiles directly to grid opcodes.

## The Sandbreak protocol

The standard quiet-breach sequence taught in underground academies is
called the Sandbreak protocol. Its four phases are: scan the subnet without
touching it, spoof a legitimate handshake, deploy the breach daemon against
the weakest ICE segment, and extract before the countermeasures
recalibrate. Running the full protocol takes a skilled netrunner under
ninety seconds. Rushing phase three is the most common cause of flatlined
runners, because black ICE reacts to brute force with overwhelming
retaliation.

## Legendary runners

The most famous netrunner of the past decade operated under the handle
Static Saint. They are credited with the only recorded solo breach of a
Vault-X tier-1 vault, an exploit that reportedly took eleven seconds and
left no logs. Static Saint vanished after the heist, and their cyberdeck is
displayed — almost certainly a replica — in the Meridian Systems museum of
computing history.
