# The Data Grid

The data grid is the city-wide network that carries everything: money,
traffic signals, firmware patches, surveillance feeds and the private
traffic of eleven million people. It succeeded the old internet after the
Collapse, and its design philosophy is the opposite of its predecessor:
the grid is physically owned, privately policed and completely
unanonymous at the transport layer.

## Physical layer

The grid's backbone is a mesh of underground fibre-optic trunks beneath
the city, with wireless relay towers filling the gaps on the surface.
Traffic is routed by the Exchange, a bank of analogue routing engines in
the Foundry district that pre-dates the Collapse and can no longer be
shut down or patched — only fed. Every packet on the grid is metered, and
the metering records are admissible evidence.

## Protocols

The grid runs on the Signal-stack: Signal/9 for transport, Signal/12 for
device discovery and Signal/0 for maintenance. Signal/0 is the interesting
one: it is an unauthenticated maintenance protocol that the Exchange
itself uses to reconfigure its routing engines. In theory, Signal/0 can
only be reached from inside the Foundry. In practice, netrunners have
spent two decades tunnelling toward it, and the protocol is the subject of
more urban legends than any other system in the city.

## The Protocol Guild

The Exchange is maintained by the Protocol Guild, a closed order of
engineers who live inside the Foundry and take a vow never to hold
corporate employment. The Guild's oath is short: "Keep the light on."
Guild members wear brass tokens stamped with a signal-wave sigil, are
granted free passage through every district checkpoint, and are the only
people in Night City whom all four megacorporations officially refuse to
bribe — because bribing the Guild would collapse the grid and everyone
knows it.

## Ghost frequencies

Between the licensed bands are the ghost frequencies: narrow slivers of
spectrum the Exchange cannot route, used by pirate radio, unlicensed
implant firmware pushes and the famous Numbers Station 7, which has
broadcast a slow sequence of primes every night since the Collapse for
reasons no one has ever explained.
